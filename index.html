07 · FAQ

Questions about building your home

What is Autonodwell?

Autonodwell is a homebuilding platform that connects your budget to custom home design, land and financing in one process. You start with what you can afford, check or find a lot, pick a home that fits, and follow it through permits, construction and move-in.

How do I start if I don’t know my budget?

Answer two questions: do you have land, and do you know your budget? “I don’t know yet” is an option for both. If you need help with both, we start with a budget range and use it to guide the lot search.

Do I need to own land to build with Autonodwell?

No. If you own a lot, we use it from the first step. If you’re looking at a lot, we check it before you buy. If you need land, your budget sets the lot price range for your search.

What does the budget include?

The preliminary budget is all-in: land, site work, the house, design, permits, utility connections, financing costs and contingency. It is a starting range, not a quote.

Can you tell me if a lot will work before I buy it?

Yes, that is the point of step two. We check parcel records, zoning, allowed use, setbacks, height, lot coverage, easements, access, slope, hazards and utility availability, and flag when a survey, soils report or utility estimate is needed.

Is the price I see final?

Not at first. Early estimates show what could still change the cost. When you approve the design and price, you see which amounts are quoted, which are still allowances, and what is excluded. If an estimate runs over budget, we revise the home or revisit the lot before you pay for a full drawing set.

Who is involved in building my home?

Depending on the step: a lender, a feasibility lead, an architect or designer, engineers, a general contractor or estimator, and trades such as plumbers. You see what is happening; the professional team coordinates the work behind it.

How does construction financing work?

Your lender checks borrowing capacity and explains construction financing early. If you own land, they check whether its equity can count. Before work starts, they review the plans, contract, appraisal and draw schedule. A preliminary affordability estimate is not mortgage preapproval; lenders make their own credit decisions.

What happens during construction?

The general contractor schedules site work, foundation, framing, rough-ins, finishes and inspections, and manages trade coordination, change orders and draw requests. You see what is happening now, what is next, and whether anything needs your decision.

What do I get when I move in?

A walkthrough, a punch list that gets resolved, your keys, and your warranty, manuals and maintenance information after final inspections and occupancy sign-off.

Is it free to start?

Yes. Exploring homes and getting a starting budget range is free, with no commitment.

Behind every step

Who does the work, from budget to keys.

You see a clear next step. Here is the professional team doing the work behind each of the seven stages.

  1. 01

    Find my budget

    Who’s involved · Autonodwell + lender

    We build a preliminary all-in budget: land, site work, the house, design, permits, utility connections, financing costs and contingency. The lender checks borrowing capacity and explains construction financing. If you own land, the lender checks whether its equity can count toward financing.

  2. 02

    Check my lot or help me find one

    Who’s involved · Feasibility lead + architect as needed

    We check parcel records, zoning, allowed use, setbacks, height, lot coverage, easements, access, slope, hazards and utility availability. We identify the likely approval path and questions for planning, building or utility staff, and flag when a survey, soils report or utility estimate is needed. This screen runs before you commit to buying a lot.

  3. 03

    Pick a home that fits

    Who’s involved · Architect or designer + contractor or estimator

    We test how the home sits on the site: entry, driveway, grading and utility connections, adapting the layout as needed. The contractor gives early pricing on the house and site work. If the estimate runs over budget, we revise the home or revisit the lot before you pay for a full drawing set.

  4. 04

    Approve the design and price

    Who’s involved · Architect + engineers + contractor

    The drawings are developed and structure, energy, plumbing, mechanical and electrical layouts are coordinated. A plumber can advise when sewer routing, connection costs or fixture placement could affect the design. The contractor gathers trade bids, reconciles them with the estimate, and identifies exclusions and allowances.

  5. 05

    Get permits and financing ready

    Who’s involved · Architect or permit lead + contractor + lender

    Plans are submitted, reviews coordinated and comments answered. In parallel, the construction contract and pricing are finalized as scope is confirmed. The lender reviews the plans, contract, appraisal and draw schedule. Permits and funding are confirmed before work starts.

  6. 06

    Build my home

    Who’s involved · General contractor + trades

    Site work, foundation, framing, rough-ins, finishes and inspections are scheduled. The plumber typically handles below-slab work if needed, rough-in before walls close, and final fixtures. The contractor manages trade coordination, change orders and draw requests.

  7. 07

    Move in

    Who’s involved · Contractor + project team

    Final inspections and occupancy sign-off are completed, punch-list items resolved, and warranties, manuals and maintenance information handed over. The lender handles any final draw or loan conversion that applies.

Why

Three reasons, none of them simple

Supply

Building fell behind demand

Underbuilding, restrictive zoning, minimum lot sizes and longer permitting limited new housing.

Cost

Building got more expensive

Construction has shown no long-run productivity growth since 1987, while land, labor and material costs rose.

Size

New homes grew 40% larger

Part of the increase buys more space. The starter home most families need largely stopped being built.

Partner FAQ

Questions from lenders, builders and partners

How do lenders fit into the Autonodwell process?

From the first step. The lender checks borrowing capacity, explains construction financing and checks whether owned land can count as equity. Before construction, the lender reviews the plans, contract, appraisal and draw schedule, and at the end handles any final draw or loan conversion.

What project information can a lender or underwriter expect?

An all-in budget, a lot feasibility screen, drawings, a construction contract and a price breakdown that separates quoted amounts from allowances and exclusions. We want to shape this package with lenders. Autonodwell outputs have not yet been validated by a lender for underwriting.

When do builders and contractors get involved?

Early. The contractor or estimator prices the house and site work while the home is being fitted to the lot, then gathers trade bids, reconciles them with the estimate and finalizes the contract as scope is confirmed. During construction, the contractor manages trades, change orders and draw requests.

What does the feasibility lead or architect do?

The feasibility lead screens a lot before purchase: zoning, setbacks, easements, access, slope, hazards and utilities, plus the likely approval path. The architect or designer tests the home on the site, develops the drawings, and often leads permit submissions and responses.

How are trades such as plumbers involved?

A plumber can advise during design when sewer routing, connection costs or fixture placement could affect it. On site, the plumber typically handles below-slab work if needed, rough-in before walls close, and final fixtures.

How does Autonodwell reduce scope ambiguity before work begins?

Budget and lot are checked before a home is picked, the home is priced on its actual site before full drawings, and the final price separates quoted amounts, allowances and exclusions. If an estimate exceeds the budget, the home or the lot is revisited first.

How is Autonodwell approaching capital?

We’re starting with the information, financing and delivery bottlenecks, not with an oversized factory. Capital is deployed against milestones, with a clear account of what has and hasn’t been proven yet.

Can developers or landowners bring a site?

Yes. Sites go through the same feasibility screen, and the delivery system is tested on individual homes before larger programs.
